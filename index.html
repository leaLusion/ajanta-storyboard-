The Elephant Who Gave His Tusks

A storyboard of the Chhaddanta Jataka, a tale of jealousy, revenge and boundless compassion, painted on the walls of the Ajanta caves and retold here as a modern visual sequence.

The storyboard

The source in stone

The Chhaddanta Jataka is painted at Ajanta in Cave 17, a fifth-century CE monastery of the Vakataka period, and an earlier version survives in Cave 10. Jataka tales recount the Buddha's earlier births, and the Ajanta artists told them as continuous narratives in which one figure appears several times within a single mural.

This storyboard keeps that spirit but cuts the continuous mural into separate panels, as a graphic novel does.

How the story was reimagined

The colours come from the natural pigments of the caves: lapis blue, yellow ochre, red ochre, green earth, lime white and lamp black. The figures use the lowered eyes and softly bent postures typical of Ajanta painting.

Each panel adds a shot note, a film-style direction of camera and framing, so that the old story reads in a modern visual language.

Illustrations are original vector drawings inspired by the Ajanta Jataka murals. Story sources: the Chhaddanta Jataka (Jataka no. 514) and published studies of the Ajanta caves.
